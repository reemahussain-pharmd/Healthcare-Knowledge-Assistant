CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINE

TITLE: Management of Hypertension in Adults
ISSUING BODY: International Hypertension Society (IHS)
DOCUMENT TYPE: Medical Guideline
VERSION: 4.0 | YEAR: 2023
DISEASE AREA: Cardiovascular / Hypertension

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Hypertension (high blood pressure) remains the most prevalent modifiable risk factor for
cardiovascular disease, stroke, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease globally, affecting
approximately 1.28 billion adults aged 30-79 years worldwide. These guidelines provide
evidence-based recommendations for the detection, evaluation, and treatment of hypertension
in adults.

SECTION 1: DEFINITIONS AND CLASSIFICATION

1.1 Blood Pressure Classification (Adults >= 18 years)

Category                  | Systolic (mmHg)  | Diastolic (mmHg)
Normal                    | < 120             | AND < 80
Elevated                  | 120-129           | AND < 80
Stage 1 Hypertension      | 130-139           | OR 80-89
Stage 2 Hypertension      | >= 140            | OR >= 90
Hypertensive Crisis       | > 180             | AND/OR > 120

1.2 Resistant Hypertension
Defined as blood pressure that remains above goal despite concurrent use of three antihypertensive
medications of different classes (including a diuretic) at optimal doses.

SECTION 2: DIAGNOSIS

2.1 Measurement Principles
- Two or more blood pressure measurements should be taken at each visit
- Patient should be seated quietly for at least 5 minutes before measurement
- Measurement in both arms recommended at initial visit; use arm with higher reading thereafter
- Confirm diagnosis with out-of-office measurement (home BP monitoring or 24-hour ambulatory BP)

2.2 White Coat Hypertension
Clinic blood pressure >= 130/80 mmHg with out-of-office readings < 130/80 mmHg.
Estimated prevalence: 15-30% of patients with elevated clinic BP.

2.3 Masked Hypertension
Normal clinic blood pressure (< 130/80 mmHg) but elevated out-of-office readings.
Associated with increased cardiovascular risk; treatment threshold same as confirmed hypertension.

SECTION 3: LIFESTYLE MODIFICATIONS

All patients with hypertension or elevated blood pressure should be counseled on:

3.1 Dietary Approaches
- DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) diet: estimated BP reduction 8-14 mmHg
- Sodium restriction to < 1500-2300 mg/day: estimated BP reduction 2-8 mmHg
- Increased potassium intake (3500-5000 mg/day through diet)
- Limit alcohol: <= 1 drink/day for women, <= 2 drinks/day for men

3.2 Physical Activity
- Aerobic exercise: >= 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity or >= 75 minutes/week vigorous
- Estimated BP reduction: 4-9 mmHg
- Dynamic resistance training: 2-3 days/week as adjunct to aerobic exercise

3.3 Weight Management
- Target BMI 18.5-24.9 kg/m2
- Weight loss reduces BP approximately 1 mmHg per kg lost

3.4 Tobacco Cessation
While smoking cessation does not directly lower resting BP, it dramatically reduces overall
cardiovascular risk and should be strongly encouraged in all hypertensive patients.

SECTION 4: PHARMACOLOGICAL TREATMENT

4.1 Treatment Initiation Thresholds

Patient Group                          | Treatment BP Threshold
Stage 2 Hypertension                   | >= 140/90 mmHg - initiate drug therapy
Stage 1 Hypertension (high CV risk)    | >= 130/80 mmHg
Stage 1 Hypertension (low CV risk)     | >= 130/80 mmHg after lifestyle modification trial
Patients with CVD, CKD, diabetes       | >= 130/80 mmHg

4.2 BP Treatment Targets

Patient Population                     | Target BP
General adults < 65 years             | < 130/80 mmHg
Adults >= 65 years                    | < 130/80 mmHg (if tolerated)
Frail elderly (individualized)        | < 150/90 mmHg
Diabetes mellitus                     | < 130/80 mmHg
Chronic kidney disease                | < 130/80 mmHg
Post-MI / High CVD risk               | < 130/80 mmHg

4.3 First-Line Antihypertensive Medications

The following drug classes are recommended as first-line therapy:

a) ACE Inhibitors (ACEI)
Examples: Lisinopril 10-40 mg/day, Ramipril 2.5-10 mg/day, Enalapril 5-40 mg/day
Preferred in: CKD with proteinuria, heart failure, post-MI, diabetes
Contraindicated in: Pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis, history of angioedema
Monitoring: Potassium, creatinine, eGFR within 2-4 weeks of initiation

b) Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARB)
Examples: Losartan 50-100 mg/day, Valsartan 80-320 mg/day, Olmesartan 20-40 mg/day
Preferred when ACEI not tolerated (e.g., cough)
Same contraindications as ACEI; do NOT combine with ACEI (increases adverse effects)

c) Calcium Channel Blockers (CCB)
Dihydropyridines: Amlodipine 5-10 mg/day, Nifedipine XL 30-90 mg/day
Non-dihydropyridines: Diltiazem, Verapamil (avoid in heart failure with reduced EF)
Preferred in: Elderly, isolated systolic hypertension, angina (dihydropyridines)

d) Thiazide/Thiazide-like Diuretics
Examples: Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ) 12.5-25 mg/day, Chlorthalidone 12.5-25 mg/day,
Indapamide 1.25-2.5 mg/day
Note: Chlorthalidone and indapamide preferred over HCTZ for cardiovascular outcomes
Monitor: Electrolytes (sodium, potassium), glucose, uric acid

4.4 Combination Therapy
Most patients with Stage 2 Hypertension require two or more agents to achieve target BP.

Preferred Combinations:
- ACEI/ARB + CCB (most commonly recommended)
- ACEI/ARB + thiazide diuretic
- CCB + thiazide diuretic
- ACEI/ARB + CCB + thiazide diuretic (triple therapy)

Avoid: ACEI + ARB combination (dual renin-angiotensin system blockade increases adverse events)

4.5 Special Populations

Elderly Patients (>= 65 years):
- Initiate with lower doses; titrate slowly
- Monitor for orthostatic hypotension
- Thiazide diuretics and CCBs generally well tolerated
- ACEI/ARB preferred if CKD or proteinuria present

Patients with Diabetes:
- ACEI or ARB preferred (renoprotective)
- Target BP < 130/80 mmHg
- Avoid high-dose thiazides (may worsen glycemic control)

Patients with Heart Failure:
- ACEI or ARB (or ARNI: sacubitril/valsartan) + beta-blocker + MRA
- Loop diuretics for fluid overload
- Avoid non-dihydropyridine CCBs in HFrEF

Patients with Chronic Kidney Disease:
- ACEI or ARB first-line (reduce proteinuria, slow progression)
- Monitor potassium closely
- Target < 130/80 mmHg

SECTION 5: MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP

5.1 Follow-up Schedule
- Within 1 month of initiating or changing therapy
- Every 3 months until BP goal achieved
- Every 6-12 months once stable

5.2 Adherence Assessment
- Medication adherence is a major cause of treatment failure
- Use pill counts, pharmacy refill records, or serum drug levels if poor control
- Motivational interviewing and simplified regimens improve adherence

5.3 Laboratory Monitoring
- Basic metabolic panel (electrolytes, creatinine, glucose) at baseline and after medication changes
- Annual fasting lipid panel and fasting glucose/HbA1c
- Urinalysis and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio annually

SECTION 6: HYPERTENSIVE URGENCY AND EMERGENCY

6.1 Hypertensive Urgency
BP > 180/120 mmHg WITHOUT acute end-organ damage.
Management: Oral medications to lower BP over 24-48 hours.
Do NOT lower BP too rapidly.

6.2 Hypertensive Emergency
BP > 180/120 mmHg WITH acute end-organ damage (encephalopathy, acute MI, pulmonary edema,
dissecting aortic aneurysm, eclampsia).
Management: IV antihypertensive therapy in ICU setting.
Target: Reduce MAP by no more than 25% in first hour.
Common IV agents: Labetalol, nicardipine, nitroprusside (if aortic dissection), hydralazine.

SECTION 7: EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Landmark Trials Referenced:
- SPRINT Trial (2015): Intensive BP control target < 120 mmHg reduced CV events by 25%
- ACCORD Trial (2010): Intensive BP control in T2DM showed no additional CV benefit
- HOPE-3 Trial (2016): BP lowering in intermediate-risk patients reduced stroke by 27%
- ONTARGET Trial (2008): Demonstrated ARB non-inferiority to ACEI
- ALLHAT Trial (2002): Chlorthalidone vs ACEI vs CCB outcomes
- VALUE Trial (2004): Amlodipine vs Valsartan in high-risk hypertension
