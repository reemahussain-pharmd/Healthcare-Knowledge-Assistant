DRUG INFORMATION DOCUMENT

DRUG NAME: Atorvastatin Calcium (Lipitor)
DRUG CLASS: HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitor (Statin)
DOCUMENT TYPE: Drug Information Document
DISEASE AREA: Cardiovascular
PREPARED BY: Medical Information Department
DATE: March 2023

1. DESCRIPTION
Atorvastatin calcium is a synthetic lipid-lowering agent belonging to the HMG-CoA reductase
inhibitor class (statins). Chemical formula: [R-(R*,R*)]-2-(4-fluorophenyl)-beta,delta-dihydroxy-
5-(1-methylethyl)-3-phenyl-4-[(phenylamino)carbonyl]-1H-pyrrole-1-heptanoic acid, calcium salt (2:1)

Molecular Formula: (C33H34FN2O5)2Ca
Molecular Weight: 1209.42 g/mol

2. MECHANISM OF ACTION
Atorvastatin is a selective, competitive inhibitor of HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme
responsible for the conversion of 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-coenzyme A (HMG-CoA) to mevalonate,
a precursor of sterols including cholesterol.

Primary effects:
- Reduces hepatic cholesterol synthesis
- Upregulates LDL receptors on hepatocytes (increases LDL clearance from blood)
- Reduces circulating LDL-C, VLDL-C, total cholesterol, triglycerides
- Increases HDL-C modestly
- Anti-inflammatory effects (reduces CRP)
- Pleiotropic effects: improved endothelial function, reduced platelet aggregation

3. INDICATIONS AND USAGE

Approved Indications:
3.1 Primary Hyperlipidemia and Mixed Dyslipidemia
To reduce elevated total-C, LDL-C, apo B, TG, and non-HDL-C, and to increase HDL-C.

3.2 Heterozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) in Adults
For treatment when dietary measures have not been adequate.

3.3 Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HoFH)
As adjunct to other lipid-lowering treatments.

3.4 Cardiovascular Risk Reduction
Primary Prevention: Reduces the risk of MI, stroke, revascularization procedures in patients
without clinically evident CHD but with multiple risk factors.
Secondary Prevention: Reduces the risk of MI, fatal/non-fatal stroke, hospitalization for CHF,
angina, and revascularization in patients with CHD.

3.5 Pediatric Use (HeFH)
Approved in pediatric patients 10-17 years with HeFH.

4. DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION

4.1 General Dosing
Starting Dose:    10 or 20 mg once daily
Usual Dose Range: 10-80 mg once daily
Maximum Dose:     80 mg once daily

Can be administered at any time of day, with or without food.

4.2 Dose Selection Based on LDL-C Goal

LDL-C Reduction Required | Starting Dose
< 25%                    | 10 mg
25-35%                   | 10-20 mg
>= 40% (high-intensity)  | 40-80 mg

High-intensity therapy (40-80 mg): Reduces LDL-C >= 50%
Moderate-intensity therapy (10-20 mg): Reduces LDL-C 30-50%

4.3 Special Populations

Renal Impairment: No dose adjustment required (renal excretion is minimal)
Hepatic Impairment: Contraindicated in active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations
of serum transaminases
Elderly (>= 65 years): No dose adjustment required; use lowest effective dose
Pediatric (10-17 years): Starting dose 10 mg/day; maximum 20 mg/day

4.4 Drug Interactions Affecting Dosing
- Cyclosporine: Atorvastatin 10 mg maximum daily dose
- Clarithromycin, itraconazole, lopinavir/ritonavir: Atorvastatin 20 mg maximum daily dose
- Nelfinavir: Atorvastatin 40 mg maximum daily dose

5. CONTRAINDICATIONS
- Active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations in serum transaminases (>3x ULN)
- Pregnancy (category X)
- Breastfeeding
- Known hypersensitivity to any component of the product

6. WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS

6.1 Skeletal Muscle Effects (Myopathy/Rhabdomyolysis)
Risk increases with:
- Higher doses (particularly 80 mg)
- Advanced age (>= 65 years)
- Hypothyroidism
- Renal impairment
- Concomitant use of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, fibrates, or niacin

Signs/Symptoms: Unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, weakness, particularly with fever or malaise.
Action: Discontinue if CK > 10x ULN, or if myopathy suspected.

6.2 Hepatic Effects
Persistent liver enzyme elevations occur in approximately 0.7% of patients.
Monitoring: Liver enzyme tests before initiating treatment and if signs/symptoms of liver injury appear.

6.3 Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM)
Rare autoimmune condition associated with statin use. May persist or relapse after discontinuation.

6.4 Diabetes Mellitus
Statin use is associated with increased risk of new-onset diabetes (~10% relative risk increase).
Benefits of statin therapy generally outweigh the risk in most patients.

7. ADVERSE REACTIONS

7.1 Common Adverse Events (incidence >= 2%)
- Nasopharyngitis: 8.3%
- Arthralgia: 6.9%
- Diarrhea: 6.8%
- Pain in extremity: 6.0%
- Urinary tract infection: 5.7%
- Dyspepsia: 4.7%
- Nausea: 4.0%
- Musculoskeletal pain: 3.8%
- Myalgia: 3.5%
- Muscle spasms: 2.8%

7.2 Laboratory Abnormalities
Creatine Kinase > 10x ULN: 0.4% with Atorvastatin vs 0.2% with placebo
Transaminase elevations (AST/ALT > 3x ULN): 0.7%

8. PHARMACOKINETICS

8.1 Absorption
Rapidly absorbed after oral administration; peak plasma concentrations achieved in 1-2 hours.
Absolute bioavailability of atorvastatin is approximately 14% (extensive first-pass hepatic extraction).

8.2 Distribution
Mean volume of distribution: approximately 381 liters
Plasma protein binding: >= 98%

8.3 Metabolism
Extensively metabolized by CYP3A4 to active ortho- and parahydroxylated derivatives.
Active metabolites contribute approximately 70% of HMG-CoA reductase inhibitory activity in vivo.

8.4 Elimination
Primary route: Bile/feces (renal excretion minimal)
Half-life of atorvastatin: approximately 14 hours
Half-life of HMG-CoA reductase inhibitory activity: approximately 20-30 hours

9. DRUG INTERACTIONS

Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (INCREASE atorvastatin levels):
- Itraconazole, ketoconazole: Increase AUC 5-fold
- Clarithromycin: Increase AUC ~4-fold
- HIV protease inhibitors (lopinavir, ritonavir, saquinavir): Increase AUC 2-6 fold

Fibrates: Combination increases myopathy risk
Niacin >= 1 g/day: Increased myopathy risk
Colchicine: Myopathy risk (use with caution)
Bile acid sequestrants: Reduce atorvastatin absorption (administer 2+ hours apart)
Digoxin: Atorvastatin 80 mg increases digoxin concentrations by 20%
Oral contraceptives: Increases AUC of norethindrone and ethinyl estradiol

10. PATIENT COUNSELING INFORMATION
- Take exactly as prescribed; do not skip doses
- Report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness immediately
- Avoid large quantities of grapefruit juice (inhibits CYP3A4)
- Avoid alcohol in excessive amounts (increases liver damage risk)
- Effective contraception required during treatment (pregnancy Category X)
- Regular liver function monitoring as advised by healthcare provider
