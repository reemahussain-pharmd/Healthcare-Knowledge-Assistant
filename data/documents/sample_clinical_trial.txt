CLINICAL TRIAL REPORT

STUDY TITLE: A Phase III, Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy
and Safety of Metformin Extended-Release (XR) 2000 mg/day in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus
and Inadequate Glycemic Control

PROTOCOL NUMBER: MET-XR-2023-001
SPONSOR: Global Clinical Research Institute
VERSION: 2.0 | DATE: January 15, 2023
DOCUMENT TYPE: Clinical Trial Report

SECTION 1: STUDY OVERVIEW

1.1 Background and Rationale
Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by insulin resistance and
progressive beta-cell dysfunction, resulting in hyperglycemia. The global prevalence of T2DM has risen
dramatically to approximately 537 million adults worldwide as of 2021, with projections reaching 784
million by 2045 (IDF Diabetes Atlas, 2021).

Metformin hydrochloride, a biguanide antihyperglycemic agent, remains the first-line pharmacological
therapy for T2DM according to the American Diabetes Association (ADA) Standards of Medical Care.
The mechanism of action primarily involves suppression of hepatic glucose production, improvement of
insulin sensitivity, and enhanced peripheral glucose uptake.

1.2 Objectives

Primary Objective:
To evaluate the efficacy of Metformin XR 2000 mg/day compared to placebo in reducing HbA1c
(glycated hemoglobin) from baseline to Week 24 in adult patients with T2DM.

Secondary Objectives:
- To assess the effect on fasting plasma glucose (FPG) levels
- To evaluate change in body weight from baseline
- To assess impact on lipid profile (total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, triglycerides)
- To evaluate overall safety and tolerability of Metformin XR

SECTION 2: STUDY DESIGN

2.1 Trial Design
This was a Phase III, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study
conducted at 45 investigational sites across 12 countries. The total study duration was 32 weeks:
- 4-week screening period
- 24-week double-blind treatment period
- 4-week follow-up period

2.2 Randomization
Eligible patients were randomized in a 2:1 ratio (Metformin XR: placebo) using a centralized
interactive web response system (IWRS). Randomization was stratified by:
- Baseline HbA1c level (7.0-8.5% vs. >8.5-10.0%)
- Prior antidiabetic medication use (naive vs. experienced)
- Geographic region

2.3 Blinding
The study was double-blind. Both patients and investigators were blinded to treatment assignment.
Identical placebo tablets were used to maintain blinding throughout the study period.

SECTION 3: STUDY POPULATION

3.1 Inclusion Criteria
Patients were eligible for enrollment if they met ALL of the following criteria:
1. Adult patients aged 18 to 75 years (inclusive)
2. Confirmed diagnosis of Type 2 diabetes mellitus for at least 6 months prior to screening
3. HbA1c between 7.0% and 10.0% at screening
4. Body mass index (BMI) between 22 and 45 kg/m2
5. Fasting plasma glucose (FPG) <= 270 mg/dL at screening
6. Stable weight (< 5% change in body weight during the 3 months prior to screening)
7. Ability to provide written informed consent
8. Willingness to comply with all study procedures and protocol requirements

3.2 Exclusion Criteria
Patients were excluded if they met ANY of the following criteria:
1. Type 1 diabetes mellitus or secondary diabetes
2. Use of insulin or GLP-1 receptor agonists within 3 months prior to screening
3. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) < 45 mL/min/1.73m2
4. Hepatic impairment (ALT or AST > 2.5 times upper limit of normal)
5. History of lactic acidosis or any condition associated with increased risk of lactic acidosis
6. Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis
7. Radiological contrast studies within 48 hours prior to or following initiation of study drug
8. Pregnancy or breastfeeding
9. Active cardiovascular disease within 6 months (myocardial infarction, stroke, unstable angina)
10. Chronic kidney disease Stage 4 or 5

SECTION 4: STUDY MEDICATIONS

4.1 Investigational Product
Drug Name: Metformin Hydrochloride Extended-Release
Dosage Form: Film-coated extended-release tablets
Strength: 500 mg per tablet
Dose: 2000 mg (4 x 500 mg tablets) once daily with the evening meal
Route: Oral

4.2 Dose Titration Schedule
- Weeks 1-2: 500 mg once daily (1 tablet)
- Weeks 3-4: 1000 mg once daily (2 tablets)
- Weeks 5-6: 1500 mg once daily (3 tablets)
- Week 7 onwards: 2000 mg once daily (4 tablets)

4.3 Concomitant Medications
Permitted: Antihypertensive agents, lipid-lowering therapy (stable for >= 3 months),
thyroid replacement therapy, aspirin (<= 325 mg/day)
Prohibited: Other antidiabetic agents, systemic corticosteroids, weight loss medications

SECTION 5: EFFICACY RESULTS

5.1 Primary Endpoint Results
The primary endpoint was change in HbA1c from baseline to Week 24.

Treatment Group    |  N   | Baseline HbA1c | Week 24 HbA1c | Mean Change | p-value
Metformin XR       | 312  |   8.4 +/- 0.9% |   7.2 +/- 0.8%  |  -1.2 +/- 0.6%  | <0.001
Placebo            | 158  |   8.3 +/- 0.8% |   8.1 +/- 0.9%  |  -0.2 +/- 0.5%  |

Treatment difference (Metformin XR vs. Placebo): -1.0% (95% CI: -1.2 to -0.8; p < 0.001)

5.2 Secondary Endpoint Results

Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG):
- Metformin XR: Mean reduction of 32.4 mg/dL (p < 0.001)
- Placebo: Mean reduction of 4.1 mg/dL

Body Weight:
- Metformin XR: Mean change -1.8 kg (95% CI: -2.3 to -1.3)
- Placebo: Mean change -0.3 kg

Proportion of patients achieving HbA1c < 7.0%:
- Metformin XR: 47.8% (n=149/312)
- Placebo: 18.4% (n=29/158)

SECTION 6: SAFETY RESULTS

6.1 Overview of Adverse Events
Any adverse event: Metformin XR 68.3% vs Placebo 54.4%
Treatment-related AEs: Metformin XR 38.5% vs Placebo 19.0%
Serious adverse events (SAEs): Metformin XR 4.8% vs Placebo 5.1%
Discontinuations due to AEs: Metformin XR 5.1% vs Placebo 2.5%

6.2 Most Common Adverse Events (incidence >= 5% in either treatment group)

Adverse Event               | Metformin XR (n=312) | Placebo (n=158)
Nausea                      | 24.0%                | 7.6%
Diarrhea                    | 19.2%                | 6.3%
Vomiting                    | 12.5%                | 3.8%
Abdominal discomfort        | 11.2%                | 5.7%
Dyspepsia                   | 9.6%                 | 4.4%
Flatulence                  | 7.7%                 | 3.2%
Headache                    | 8.7%                 | 9.5%
Nasopharyngitis             | 7.4%                 | 8.2%
Upper respiratory infection | 5.8%                 | 6.3%

6.3 Serious Adverse Events
No cases of lactic acidosis were reported in either treatment group.
Two deaths occurred during the study: one in the Metformin XR group (unrelated to study drug -
traffic accident) and one in the placebo group (cardiac failure).

6.4 Laboratory Findings
No clinically meaningful changes in renal function (eGFR, serum creatinine) were observed.
Vitamin B12 levels showed a modest decline in the Metformin XR group (mean -18 pg/mL vs +2 pg/mL
for placebo), consistent with known mechanism of Metformin on B12 absorption.

SECTION 7: CONCLUSIONS

Metformin XR 2000 mg/day demonstrated statistically significant and clinically meaningful
improvements in glycemic control compared to placebo in adult patients with T2DM and
inadequate glycemic control:
- HbA1c reduction of 1.0% greater than placebo (p < 0.001)
- 47.8% of patients achieved HbA1c < 7.0% vs 18.4% with placebo
- Significant reductions in FPG and body weight

The safety profile was consistent with the well-established profile of Metformin, with
gastrointestinal adverse events being the most common. No new safety signals were identified.
The results support the use of Metformin XR 2000 mg/day as an effective and well-tolerated
treatment option for T2DM patients requiring additional glycemic control.
